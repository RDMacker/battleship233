# Content Think Tank: GSMBC — 2026-10-20

The fortnight handed GSMBC more usable material than most seasons do. Asa Vermette, nineteen years old and riding for Neko Mulally's small Frameworks program, clinched the elite downhill World Cup overall at Lake Placid on October 4 and then won Red Bull Hardline's first North American edition on October 17 after Jackson Goldstone crashed out of a 1.7 second lead, while Rampage's 25th edition ended with no men's champion after the October 10 wind cancellation and Robin Goomes completed a historic three-peat in the women's event. Closer to the business, the audience is arguing about coaching legitimacy itself: Pinkbike comment sections are openly calling the certification industry out, the Apogee Academy app (version 1.0.7 shipped October 5) has made app-based coaching a mainstream category, and the Singletrack forums keep repeating one complaint, that clinic gains fade because nobody designs the practice that follows. The formats break our way too, since Socialinsider's Q3 2026 benchmarks put carousels ahead of Reels on engagement for the first time, Ahrefs' February 2026 study found AI Overviews cutting clicks to top pages by 58% so the blog's job is now citations rather than traffic, and the measurable backlash against AI-generated filler makes a deep, authored prose voice a commercial asset. The room's strongest work turned those three live arguments, legitimacy, retention, and the app, into positions only GSMBC can hold.

## The slate

### Blog

**Look Where You Want to Go Is a Description, Not an Instruction** (flagship essay, boldness 3). The highest-scoring idea in the room attacks the most repeated cue in mountain biking: it describes what expert gaze already does and hands the learner nothing to practice, which is why it evaporates at the exact moment of fear it was supposed to fix. The essay opens on the emtbforums rider who, after years of hearing the advice, still stares white-knuckled at the midpoint of every skinny bridge (the anecdote merged in from insider-3's duplicate pitch), brings in Joan Vickers' quiet eye work conversationally, shows the constrained practice tasks that actually train gaze, then generalizes: most popular MTB cues are descriptions of expert output dressed up as inputs. The hook is the forum confession itself, a thread full of riders admitting the famous advice fails precisely when they need it. Why now: that thread is live this month, and the flood of generic drill content makes a researched takedown of generic cueing both timely and unscrapeable. All three critics called it the flagship and said to write it before anything else.

**The Drill Audit: We Go First** (quarterly series, boldness 4). Imports evidence-based lifting's public program audit into MTB, one widely taught drill per episode examined against motor learning research for what it actually trains versus what it only appears to train, and the move that makes it publishable is episode one, which audits a drill from GSMBC's own curriculum and names where the research has moved since it was written. Each episode follows a fixed structure (what the drill asks of the body, what transfers, what to keep, change, or retire) so it reads as method rather than takedown. The hook: there is a drill in our own curriculum the research has been quietly arguing with for three years, so before we audit anyone else's teaching, we are going to audit ours. Why now: Milo Wolf's March 2026 audit of Jeff Nippard's program proved the format builds authority for both sides, and the AI slop backlash is pushing readers toward named, accountable analysis. Critics said the self-audit opening converts the scariest idea on the slate into the most credible, killed the competitor-first version (maverick-2), and advised writing episode one before promising a series.

**The Six Weeks After the Clinic** (essay plus Monday newsletter edition, boldness 1). The answer to the most repeated complaint paying customers make, that the day itself works and the gains fade because nobody designs what follows. It opens with the Singletrack rider who called his coaching day a day of strategic practice (that framing merged in from insider-2) and priced it against a carbon bar, then teaches coaches to build the post-session plan using spacing and variability research, with a concrete six-week structure for rear wheel lifts a coach can lift straight into client follow-up, and thought-leader-5's subject line, your clinic worked for exactly eleven days, carries the email edition. Why now: two active Singletrack threads keep converging on the same verdict, which is documented in-market demand nobody is answering. Critics ranked it the winner of a four-pitch cluster because the liftable template is what makes coaches forward it.

**You Cannot Constrain Your Way Out of Coaching** (essay, boldness 5). The contrarian piece aimed at the enlightened orthodoxy rather than the obvious straw men: the constraints-led approach is being wrecked by its own fans, because a radical constraint that makes the right movement the only possible movement has solved the problem for the athlete. It names Keith Davids respectfully, sides with the 2026 practitioner correction, and draws the line on a real trail between a constraint that forces discovery and the Instagram version where cones have replaced judgment. The hook is the image of a corner so coned-up there is only one line a bike can take, because the honest answer is that the cones are doing the riding. Why now: the correction is live in the skill acquisition literature and untouched in MTB. Critics called it narrow but aimed precisely at the certification-buying audience, with a line coaches will quote for years.

**The 1,200 Day Streak Problem** (essay, boldness 4). Opens inside Duolingo's 2026 backlash with the review that cut through everything, a 1,200 day streak and still no conversational Spanish, then turns the mirror on riding, where laps, Strava days, and saddle time are the same streak metrics, counting exposure while measuring nothing about learning. It closes with what a retention or transfer test looks like on a trail and how a coach designs sessions that pass them. Why now: the streak critique peaked through 2026 and sits right next to the forums' fading-gains complaint. Critics said the concrete trail-level payoff keeps it from being a borrowed meme, with one condition, verify the review is still citable before building on it.

### Pinkbike

**The Paper Is Not the Coach: What a Certification Actually Proves, From People Who Sell One** (feature, boldness 4). The definitive version of an idea the room pitched three times (analyst-3 and insider-1 merged in, including the three-questions close): a certificate proves a coach was assessed against a standard on a specific day, it predicts a floor and never a ceiling, and the industry has earned the eye-rolls by letting entry-level coaches oversell the paper. It names what certifications test, what they mostly do not (diagnosing a real learner, progressing a plateaued adult, knowing when to stay silent), stakes a position on what recertification should test instead, and ends with three questions a rider should ask any coach before paying. The hook: when Pinkbike announced the first US outfit with two Level 4 instructors, the top comment said the industry is certifying each other into oblivion, and the awkward thing for a company that runs certifications is that the commenter was half right. Why now: the legitimacy argument is running in Pinkbike comments this month with no credible insider voice in it. Critics scored it highest of the whole slate's Pinkbike pitches because the self-incrimination is the part competitors cannot copy.

**The App Will Show You the Technique, Then You Will Practice the Wrong Thing Perfectly** (feature, boldness 3). The single app piece distilled from four pitches, now opening with analyst-4's donated scene: a rider films her corner from the one angle her phone allows, the app's coach flags her braking, and nobody notices the real error happened three seconds earlier in where she looked on entry, because that error is invisible on video. From there it maps the boundary generously, what Apogee handles well (models, structure, strength work) and what no app can do (catch the error the rider cannot feel, redesign the task mid-session, decide when silence teaches more than a cue), giving every coach the answer their clients are about to ask for. Why now: Apogee shipped 1.0.7 on October 5 to warm reception, so the question is mainstream this month. Critics called the frame, everything in coaching that can become content now has, and what remains is what riders were paying for, the most quotable move of the cycle, and sequenced insider-5's reply-mining email to land the same week.

**Inside the Session Plan: Why the Cones Moved Two Metres** (feature series, boldness 3). Does for a coaching session what The Athletic's tactics writers do for a match: it breaks down the coach's decisions, not the athlete's performance, walking one real session beat by beat, anchored on the rider's problem and transformation with the coach's decision as the plot twist. MTB media has analysed riders for thirty years and never once analysed a coach, so the lane is empty, and the depth answers the certification skeptics by demonstration rather than argument. The hook: at 9:40 on a wet Saturday in Squamish, four riders were cornering fine and learning nothing, so the coach moved the cones two metres closer to the entry and said less than before, and that small decision is the whole lesson. Why now: the legitimacy debate wants proof of what coaching talent concretely looks like. Critics advised written rider consent and pitching episode one as a standalone before committing to a cadence.

### Instagram

**The Coach's Move: A Puzzle You Solve Before Slide Nine Tells You the Answer** (recurring carousel, boldness 4). Chess tactics puzzles imported to coaching: slide one presents a real position (a rider locks the rear wheel on the same corner three runs in a row, here is what she says afterwards, here is the terrain), middle slides lay out four defensible interventions, and the final slides give GSMBC's move with the motor learning reasoning, then prompt the save. Coaches argue in the comments between hook and answer, which is the engagement engine chess has run on for a century, and every puzzle is visibly original teaching material of the kind Instagram's April 2026 originality rules now reward. Why now: carousels lead Q3 2026 engagement with roughly nine times the saves of single images. Critics called it the best Instagram idea by a distance and said to start it as an occasional series sourced from sessions Rich has already coached, so a busy month cannot break a public promise.

**Saddle Time Is a Streak, Not a Skill** (eight-slide carousel, boldness 4). The carousel edition of the streak essay, one idea per slide: ride days are attendance metrics, repetition without difficulty stops producing adaptation, and the test of learning is whether it survives a week and transfers to a different trail, closing with a saveable one-screen practice audit. Slide one pairs her 1,200 day streak with your 400 ride days and the same wrong braking point, which critics called the sharpest opening slide in the slate. Why now: run it the same week as the blog so the borrow reads as a campaign, and confirm the review is quotable before putting the number on slide one.

**The Schedule Carousel, Rebuilt** (monthly template, boldness 1, merged from three pitches). Analyst-5's claim-first structure absorbs producer-5's region-vote closer and insider-6's geography module: slide one carries an arguable claim with a number, the mechanism unfolds one idea per slide with an original diagram, the course schedule sits mid-deck, one slide answers the where-do-courses-run question with real registration behavior, and the vote slide runs only in months where the winning region is guaranteed dates. Why now: the schedule post has to exist anyway, carousels are the algorithm-favored format, and the marginal cost is near zero. Critics' conditions are folded in: the retention number on slide one must survive a coach asking for the source, and the vote must be honored or not run.

### Email

**One Argument Per Send, Signed by Rich** (format repositioning, boldness 1). Every Monday and Thursday send becomes one claim Rich will defend, argued in five or six connected paragraphs, ending in exactly one action, with a recurring Thursday segment testing one circulating piece of skills advice against the evidence, calmly and by claim rather than by creator, which builds a reply habit and feeds the topic pipeline. Why now: 2026 data shows founder-voiced sends beating brand digests, and GSMBC already owns the cadence and the expert. The operator called it the cheapest idea on the slate with the highest compounding return, and said to do it first; the skeptic's note, that the Thursday evidence-check segment is the real idea, is now its centerpiece.

**The Six Weeks After: a Newsletter That Is Itself the Practice Plan** (automated sequence, boldness 4). Twelve sends over six weeks where the format is the pedagogy: each email arrives on a spaced schedule and opens with a retrieval prompt about the previous send, so a subscriber experiences spacing and retrieval practice in their inbox instead of reading an essay about it, and a coach watches a worked example they can run for their own clients. Per the operator's fix, it is built once as an opt-in automated sequence on its own list, offered after each clinic season, so the main cadence keeps running. Why now: it is the deliverable answer to the forums' retention complaint and the strongest soft certification pitch on the slate, since any coach who finishes it has felt what designed practice does. Critics named it the winner of the retention cluster because no other pitch can say the medium demonstrates the claim.

**Where the App Ends and the Coach Begins** (Monday send, boldness 3). The email spoke of the Apogee Pinkbike feature, published the same week: one argument naming the three things a screen cannot do, one call to action inviting coaches to reply with how they are answering the app question with their own clients, which turns a single send into a Thursday follow-up and a bank of real coach objections. Critics advanced it on the condition it rides the feature's news cycle rather than restating the argument alone.

## Wildcards worth the risk

**Project Plateau: We Are Coaching One Stuck Rider for Six Weeks, in Public, Failures Included** (email serial, boldness 5). The operator voted to kill it as undeliverable, and the operator is not wrong that recruiting, coaching, and documenting a real rider across six weeks could consume a founder, but the skeptic scored it 9 and called it the only idea in the slate nobody else in MTB coach education would dare run, because a failures-included serial is structurally unfakeable in a feed full of synthetic advice. The risk is operational, not editorial, so the mitigation is casting: pick the rider before announcing anything, run it on the opt-in list rather than the main cadence, and accept that if week three flops, that email is the most valuable one in the series. If it lands, it is the single strongest proof of the certification's whole premise, pedagogy operating on a rider who looks like the paying market instead of like Loïc Bruni.

**What Makes This Run Great, Episode One: Vermette at Hardline BC** (carousel pilot, boldness 3, absorbing maverick-1's written version). Two critics flagged real problems: pressure timing and decision points cannot be known from broadcast footage, and a monthly diagram-heavy episode is a serious load for one founder. But the core insight survives the criticism, the swipe is the isolation, each thumb-advance peels back one layer of a run the way Rick Beato mutes a stem, which video cannot do, and nobody in MTB deconstructs riding this way. The risk is overclaiming and overcommitting; the mitigation is to pilot exactly one episode framed as what a coach's eye sees in the footage, restrict every slide to what is literally visible, promise no series, and let saves decide whether episode two exists. The Vermette and Goldstone contrast from October 17 gives episode one a hook the whole sport just watched.

**Ten Drills to Fix Your Cornering, Written by a Machine, Marked by a Coach** (blog, boldness 5). Both the skeptic and the advocate called the expert-grades-the-AI frame a stale 2026 stunt, and they are right about the frame while the payload underneath is pure GSMBC: the machine's drills are nearly indistinguishable from most human skills content because generic technique advice is precisely what both produce. The advocate's fix turns the staleness into the weapon, run it as a blind test where readers guess which drills came from the machine and which from popular human content, then reveal, so the audience springs the trap on itself. The risk is that it reads as a gimmick anyway; the upside is a position piece on the AI wave that never whines about AI and that no summary engine can replicate, deliverable in a day.

## Killed, and why

Almost nothing died for being a bad idea; it died for being the third or fourth copy of a good one. The room converged on the certification-skeptics piece three times, the app-boundary piece four times, the clinic-retention argument four times, and the schedule-carousel rebuild three times, so the kills were mostly the weaker twins of ideas that survived, and the fix applied throughout this report was to merge each cluster into its strongest version and donate the duplicates' best assets (the bridge anecdote, the invisible-error scene, the eleven-days subject line) to the survivor. The remaining deaths follow two patterns worth remembering. First, ideas that require evidence the brand does not have: a braking map of Vermette's run implies telemetry nobody owns, and a format whose magic is audible stems does not survive translation into prose, so test every borrowed format against what GSMBC can actually show. Second, ideas that pick a fight the brand cannot win: auditing a named competitor's drill first, from a certification body with commercial skin in the game, reads as a takedown however carefully it is claimed, and the survivable version always starts with auditing ourselves. Future sessions should also stop pitching platform best practices as creative ideas; a carousel structure or an AEO page shape is plumbing, and it was counted here as plumbing.

## If you only make three things this month

**Look Where You Want to Go Is a Description, Not an Instruction**, because it is the flagship: one writer, zero dependencies, a live forum thread as evidence, and a closing generalization that is GSMBC's entire pedagogy case in a single argument coaches will forward to coaches.

**The Paper Is Not the Coach** on Pinkbike, because the legitimacy argument is happening right now without a credible insider voice, and a certification body conceding what the skeptics got right is the one move no competitor can copy and no comment section can dismiss.

**The Coach's Move** on Instagram, because the puzzle gap between slide one and slide nine manufactures comments, the answer manufactures saves, every episode is unscrapeable original teaching, and the raw material already exists in sessions Rich has coached.
