# Content Think Tank: GSMBC, November 10, 2026

The off-season opened with structures breaking. The men's Red Bull Rampage was cancelled mid-event by wind on October 12, leaving no champion for the first time and igniting the loudest risk argument of the year. Asa Vermette, 19, became the youngest elite men's downhill overall champion at Lake Placid (October 2 to 4), then won the inaugural Hardline BC at Cypress on October 16 in front of 10,000 people on GSMBC's own North Shore. The Enduro World Cup is dead after 2026, Ghost went insolvent on November 1 and was bought back by November 6, and the confirmed 2027 calendar ends the downhill season at Whistler for the first time. The platform data points the same direction as the channel mix GSMBC already runs: Socialinsider has carousels edging Reels on reach (4.50 against 4.10 percent) and dominating saves, Ahrefs tied Google AI Overviews to a 58 percent click-through decline in a February study, HubSpot's 2026 research puts perspective-led newsletter sends well ahead of digests, and the off-season skills lane in search is sitting empty, stocked with pieces one to five years old. The slate below leans into all of it: planning-season blog essays, debate-shaped Pinkbike features, and save-built carousels, with nothing hung on video.

## The slate

### Blog

**A Smoother Trail Is a Worse Teacher** (blog essay, boldness 4)
The trail-sanitization fight is being argued entirely on advocacy grounds, and this essay attacks it from the one unoccupied direction: the trail itself is the curriculum, and the variability a janky North Shore trail forces on a rider is precisely the varied practice that motor learning research says builds adaptable skill, so smoothing a trail does not make riding safer, it defers the lesson to a day with more speed and higher consequences. The credible position is not that every trail should be hard but that a network needs a deliberate spread of difficulty the way a practice plan does. Hook: "There is a root section on Fromme that has taught more riders how to manage grip than any clinic ever run on the mountain, and the current plan for trails like it is to remove the teacher." Why now: Pinkbike's sanitization piece and its contentious thread are live this season, and NSMBA's Jank and Flow campaign runs November 1 to January 31, putting the question in GSMBC's backyard during this exact window. Critics: all three advanced it, with the operator calling it the best thought-leadership piece on the slate, an unoccupied angle argued from home trails with zero production dependencies.

**Standing Above the Feature You Cleared Last Year** (blog essay, boldness 2)
A sports psychology essay on post-crash fear that opens on a rider stopped at the top of a rock roll she rode thirty times last summer, brakes locked, unable to explain why today is different, then explains threat appraisal, why the brain re-rates a feature after a crash even when skill has not changed, and why white-knuckling through it often deepens the association. It replaces "just send it eventually" with a named three-session graded exposure protocol, framed explicitly as practice design rather than therapy so it stays coaching and not clinical advice. Hook: "She cleared this rock roll maybe thirty times last summer, and today she is standing at the top of it with her brakes locked and her heart going, listening to a friend say just get through it, which is not a plan, it is a hope." Why now: the "Just get through it" Substack essay is circulating this fall and the advice landscape around fear remains mechanism-free, while off-season is when riders privately decide whether next year is the year. Critics: unanimous advance and the slate's quiet favorite, called the sleeper hit with the highest real-engagement ceiling, the piece riders send to a friend.

**The Rider Who Spent £400 on Coaching and Kept None of It** (blog essay, boldness 4, merged from insider-1, analyst-5 and thought-leader-2)
The canonical version of the concede-the-skeptics essay, which the room pitched three times. It opens by agreeing with the Singletrack forum almost verbatim, then explains the £400 rider's experience from the retention literature: a Saturday clinic produces gains that decay without spaced practice because the industry has been selling acquisition and calling it retention, which is the merged-in line with teeth. It closes by describing what a session designed for the following month does differently and sets a quiet standard riders can hold against any coach, including GSMBC's own certified alumni, which is what makes it read as honest rather than as marketing judo. Hook: "Somewhere on the Singletrack forum right now there is a rider who spent four hundred pounds on skills coaching, rode better for two weekends, and is back to exactly where he started, and the uncomfortable thing for everyone in my profession is that his story is the normal one." Why now: the "is coaching worth it" threads are live again this off-season and November is when coaching goes into next season's budget or does not. Critics: the operator scored this version a 9 and all three agreed it should run once, carrying the best parts of its two duplicates.

**The First Position Stand on Mountain Bike Skills Practice** (blog essay, boldness 4)
GSMBC borrows the most authoritative format in exercise science and grades rider folklore ("just ride more," "park laps build skill," "you cannot learn in a parking lot") against the motor learning evidence on spacing, variability, and blocked-then-varied practice, pegged to the ACSM publishing its first resistance training position stand in sixteen years. Per the rework, it is scoped to five or six graded positions rather than a full audit, the pomp is dialed down so the folklore grading carries the piece, and the ACSM peg gets verified before the hook hangs on it. Each position seeds a future Monday newsletter take, so one essay feeds a quarter. Hook: "This year the ACSM read 137 systematic reviews covering thirty thousand lifters and concluded that training to failure, the most repeated rule in every gym, is not required, and mountain bike skills practice has never once been audited that way, so this is the first attempt." Why now: the position stand is circulating through fitness media and November is when riders consume planning content. Critics: no kill votes, with the skeptic noting the format forces falsifiable positions, the rarest thing in the genre.

**The GSMBC Memo, No. 1: Where Do Coaches Come From When the Teams Disappear?** (blog essay with quarterly franchise intent, boldness 3)
A long contrarian essay in the Howard Marks mold, arguing that rider knowledge dies with the factory ride unless a certification pathway exists to capture it, written from this exact month: Ghost insolvent and rescued in five days, the Enduro World Cup dead, Laurie Greenland without a team, two squads folding. Per the critics' rework, the essay earns the franchise rather than announcing it: No. 1 ships as a complete piece, No. 2 goes on the calendar for February, and the numbering proves itself if readers wait for it. Hook: "Laurie Greenland spent a decade inside the top ten in the world, and this winter nobody is paying him to ride a bike, which tells you something about where the sport stores its knowledge." Why now: the contraction is the sport's loudest story and owned-channel long-form is the defensible asset as Google referral traffic falls. Critics: advanced by skeptic and operator, who noted quarterly cadence is honest about founder capacity, while the advocate's warning (nobody waits for memo No. 2 from a company they just met) is answered by writing the essay first.

**Winter Term: What a Parking Lot in December Teaches That a Bike Park in July Cannot** (blog series, boldness 2, merged from producer-4 and analyst-1)
The winter-skills lane, claimed with a syllabus instead of a stream, cut from six modules to three per the operator so one founder can ship it: three dated essays across November to January, each under a question-shaped heading with a plain 40 to 60 word answer block near the top so AI engines cite GSMBC, the full show-then-explain essay underneath, and a matching save-engineered carousel per module. Riders enroll via the newsletter, and the same modules re-run every winter with updates. Hook: "It is raining sideways on the Shore, the trails are closed in your head if not on the map, and the empty parking lot behind the rec centre is quietly the best motor-learning laboratory you will touch all year." Why now: it is explicitly planning month, competing off-season skills content in search is one to five years stale, and nothing fresh exists for November 2026. Critics: the room agreed the lane is empty and this container wins it; the only fight was scope, now settled at three modules with room to expand next winter.

### Pinkbike

**The Most Skilled Thing That Happened at Rampage Was Nobody Riding** (Pinkbike feature, boldness 4, merged from maverick-1, thought-leader-1 and analyst-6)
The room's strongest Pinkbike idea, assembled from its three Rampage pitches: thought-leader-1's stake and headline carrying maverick-1's aviation framework. Aviation already solved the problem freeride is arguing about, naming the failure mode (get-there-itis, the sunk-cost pull that kills experienced pilots at fogged-in strips) and building personal minimums, written go/no-go criteria a pilot sets while calm so the decision is already made when adrenaline and sponsors are watching. The piece walks the October 12 wind hold through the FAA's framework, reads Godziek's relief and van Steenbergen's frustration as both predicted by the literature, and closes on the everyday rider above a feature on Fromme, writing minimums for their own ceiling. Hook: "On October 12, nine of the best riders alive stood on a ridgeline in Virgin, Utah, watched the wind move through the flags, and the most important thing any of them did that day was nothing." Why now: the cancellation is the dominant MTB conversation of the off-season, Pinkbike ran the Todd Barber interview in early November, and nobody in the backlash is covering decision science. Critics: 8.3 with no kill votes, the one Rampage pitch with a concrete borrowed doctrine, with a standing instruction to verify the aviation specifics because pilots read Pinkbike comments too.

**What the App Can't See When It Watches You Corner** (Pinkbike feature, boldness 3)
Grounded in the audience's own verdict on AI coaching, this piece grants that the sensor is usually right about what happened, then argues from motor learning that feedback is the smallest part of coaching: the app cannot decide which of three errors matters this month, cannot read that stiffness is fear rather than technique, and cannot design the next constraint or know when to shut up. It ends by naming what AI tools are legitimately good for, which the critics flagged as load-bearing rather than optional. Hook: "The app on your handlebars watched your corner and scored it 7.4, and the number is probably accurate, which is exactly the problem, because there were three errors in that corner and only one of them is worth fixing this month, and the app has no idea which." Why now: Zwift+ launched AI coaching in late October to open skepticism, the MTBCoach app is in riders' hands, and riders testing ChatGPT self-coaching are publicly reporting it falls apart. Critics: unanimous advance, called the strongest pro-coaching argument available that never says we're experts, with the best single opening on the slate.

**The Case for the Bike, The Case for the Rider (Both Writers Are Me)** (Pinkbike feature stunt, boldness 5)
Two full essays in one feature, the strongest case that modern geometry papers over skill deficits and the strongest case that it does not, each argued with named skill-transfer evidence, with a mid-piece reveal that one coach wrote both. The closing section names which errors long-and-slack geometry hides and which it only postpones until the speed goes up, and hands the resolution to the comments. Per the operator's condition, editor buy-in on the format comes before the five thousand words get written, with the blog held as fallback, and the reveal lands early enough to feel like a trick played on the debate rather than on the reader. Hook: a cold open on two riders on identical trails, one on a 2016 trail bike cleaning everything and one on a 2026 superbike getting bucked, followed by the line "one of the two essays below is wrong, and I wrote both of them." Why now: Vital's bike-or-rider and longer-and-slacker megathreads are active this week and the 32-inch wheel experiment is restarting the argument the 26-to-29 transition already answered. Critics: no kill votes, a stunt with a reason to exist, and a one-off by design until the first one lands.

**What the Coach Sees: The Crash Started Three Seconds Earlier** (Pinkbike feature, boldness 2, merged from producer-1, insider-2, analyst-2 and maverick-2's protocol)
The room pitched the Friday Fails idea four times; this is the single surviving feature. It quotes the top comment under a recent episode verbatim, then walks the error chain backwards through prose-described clips (which dodges footage rights) to show the brake grab everyone screenshots was the fourth link, not the first, validating the commenters about the last link before handing them the rest of the chain. It carries one paragraph of the esports one-topic-per-session rule and closes with a protocol for reviewing your own GoPro footage. Per the operator, it ships as one feature and lets the editors ask for the series, which is how series actually get commissioned. Hook: "The top comment called it a fistful of brake, and the top comment was half right, because the brake grab was the fourth mistake in that clip and the first one happened three seconds earlier, back where nobody was looking." Why now: Friday Fails #428 ran November 6 and Pinkbike is soliciting community fails for season 4 of Cathro's How To Bike, so the error-analysis appetite is proven and current. Critics: every reviewer agreed only one version should exist; this is that version, carrying the best mechanic from each duplicate.

### Instagram

**One Corner, Annotated** (carousel, boldness 5)
Chess annotation culture applied frame by frame to one rider through one North Shore corner: twelve slides in the camera-roll aesthetic, trailside photos with symbols scrawled in marker, where the ?? lands two seconds before the corner on an innocuous vision move and the final slides replay the engine line a coach would have the rider play instead. Per the operator's rework, it pilots once with phone stills of a friend, explains the symbols by slide two for readers with zero chess literacy, and only becomes a monthly series after the pilot proves the photo logistics. Hook: slide one is a photo of a rider entering an unremarkable flat corner with two question marks scrawled on it and the line "this is the move that lost the corner, and it happened two seconds before the corner." Why now: carousels beat Reels on reach and saves, the late-2026 aesthetic has moved to handwritten looseness over branded template boxes, and the fails comment culture proves the audience wants to diagnose riding but only knows the last visible error. Critics: called the best Instagram idea on the slate, built for the save and the argument, with the hidden photo shoot named as the one real dependency.

**The Park Laps Argument, Settled With Specifics** (carousel, boldness 2)
A 10 to 12 slide save-built deck that steps into the argument riders have had in Pinkbike's park polls all year and answers with specificity instead of a side: what high-repetition lift-served riding trains well, what it quietly untrains (line reading on unfamiliar terrain, the variability learning depends on), and the one habit that changes the ratio, riding one trail blind or changing one line per lap. Both camps find their position partly confirmed, which is the tag-a-friend engine. The Cypress announcement gets confirmed before the final slide is built around it. Hook: slide one reads "Fifty park laps made you fast on a trail you have memorized. Here is what they quietly untrained, and the one habit that changes the ratio." Why now: Cypress just confirmed the North Shore's first lift-accessed bike park since 2005 for summer 2027, making the question local and immediate in GSMBC's home terrain. Critics: unanimous advance, Canva-native, concrete, and durable rather than merely topical.

**The Skills Iceberg** (carousel, boldness 4)
A 14-slide deck descending from what every rider knows (brake before the corner) through what good riders do (pressure timing, sequencing) down to what almost nobody practices (external focus cues, variable practice structure, designed error exposure), with the waterline drawn on slide four. Each submerged layer maps to a planned blog essay, so one afternoon of design work becomes the table of contents for a quarter of editorial. The skeptic's objection (the meme is aging) is answered by the conversion line and by making every placement defensible, because the argument in the comments is the distribution, and the deck is complete rather than a teaser. Hook: a stark iceberg graphic with "Everything above the waterline is what you already practice. Everything below it is why you are not getting faster. Almost nobody trains past slide four." Why now: longer decks get re-served by the algorithm with peak reach deep in the swipe, and nobody in MTB coaching is using the format. Critics: one kill vote on meme fatigue against an operator 8 for the zero-video, quarter-seeding economics; it stays because its job is structural, not viral.

### Email

**The Opening Take: Rebuilding Monday and Thursday Around One Paragraph of Opinion** (newsletter format change, boldness 2, merged from producer-6 and analyst-4)
Every send now opens with 120 to 180 words of Rich's take on one live MTB story connected to a single coaching mechanism, before any blog pointer, judged on click-to-open rather than opens since auto-fetch has made open rates noise. The takes that drive the most clicks become the next quarter's essays and memos, which makes the newsletter the brand's cheapest idea-testing instrument. November supplies the material: the first installment is the Zwift opener, the AI-coaching send sequenced to land after the Pinkbike feature runs so the click-through has somewhere to go, with the Honda RN0 mass-and-braking take and Squad Enduro's group-learning angle queued behind it, and the Ghost solvency line held for a week it fits a reader-first frame. Hook, first installment: "Zwift launched an AI coach two weeks ago, and the most interesting thing about it is not what it gets wrong, it is what it cannot see, because a sensor that scores your corner has no idea which of your errors to ignore this session." Why now: HubSpot's 2026 research says personal opinion drives the highest engagement while the second week of November is unusually dense with take-ready stories. Critics: the cheapest high-leverage change on the slate, advanced across the board once the two duplicate pitches merged into this spec.

**A $6,200 Bike or a $6,200 Rider** (Monday newsletter take, boldness 3)
The price fight has receipts on both sides, and this send does the arithmetic the comment sections never do, pricing a season of coaching against a single drivetrain or suspension upgrade and naming the mechanism that makes the comparison unfair: the component depreciates on a bike you will sell while braking skill and line choice transfer to every bike you will ever own and compound each season. Per the advocate's condition, it names plainly what coaching cannot fix, because without that the priced-out reader calls it an ad. The Pinkbike version of this argument was killed; in owned email the self-interest is priced in and the arithmetic lands. Hook, subject line "A $6,200 bike or a $6,200 rider," opening: "Singletracks says a mid-range trail bike now costs more than sixty-two hundred dollars, another Singletracks piece says bikes have never been cheaper, and both of them are arguing about the wrong purchase." Why now: the dueling Singletracks analyses and Pinkbike's $1,000 Budget Bike Field Test from the November Sedona festival have made cost the loudest argument of the off-season. Critics: advocate and operator advanced it as the right argument in the right channel, siding with riders who feel priced out rather than with the industry.

## Wildcards worth the risk

**Asa Vermette Is Not a Talent Story** (Pinkbike feature, boldness 3). The critics' worry is real: a founder cannot reconstruct a teenager's development environment from a desk, the deliberate-practice framing was publicly complicated a decade ago, and if the tone reads as diminishing a well-liked kid the thread turns on the author. The fix is also real: do the interview, or reframe to what high-rep environments like Durango share with Vermette as the admiring opening example, and engage the genetics-and-sampling counterevidence head on. Worth the risk because it is the sharpest contrarian stake in the room aimed at the defining performance story of 2026, and the version that survives those fixes is the piece everyone invested in the talent myth has to fight in the comments, which is the point.

**We Reviewed Friday Fails the Way an Esports Team Reviews a Lost Match** (Pinkbike feature, boldness 4). The operator voted to kill it because Pinkbike's older-skewing audience may bounce off a Valorant conceit written by someone fluent in one subculture pretending fluency in another. But the skeptic scored it an 8 for the one quality the other fail pitches lacked, a real and checkable format theft that flatters the audience instead of lecturing it. The play: the merged fail feature above ships first with one paragraph of the esports one-topic rule inside it, and if that piece lands and the comments bite, this full-costume version runs as the sequel with the VOD protocol intact. The risk is tourism; the upside is owning self-review culture in MTB before anyone else notices it exists.

**The November Transfer Window** (newsletter arc, boldness 4). All three critics said the same thing: compress the pundit register to a single Greenland-pegged Monday because the costume exhausts across a multi-send arc. They are probably right, which is why the single send is the default. The wildcard is running the full two-week arc anyway, squad audit on Mondays, signings and releases on Thursdays, because the skills-as-a-roster frame is the off-season planning content every rider needs wearing a format no coaching brand has ever worn, and if the register holds it becomes the most memorable email run of the winter and an annual franchise. The risk is a joke that dies by send three in front of the list that pays the bills; the exit is clean, since the arc collapses back to the single Monday the moment click-to-open sags.

## Killed, and why

The clearest pattern in the kills was duplication: the room pitched the Friday Fails idea four times, the Rampage decision piece three times, the coaching-skeptics essay three times, and the AI-coaching take three times, and in every case the kills landed on the copies that had the topic without the mechanism, which is why the survivors above are merges rather than picks. The second pattern was costume over cargo: the insolvency-report essay and the full-length pundit register died because an imported structure has to do mechanical work the way personal minimums or chess annotation do, and when it only decorates, the conceit exhausts itself by paragraph four and starts fighting the brand's own voice rules. Third, self-interest on rented ground: the Pinkbike version of coaching-beats-components was killed on sight because a coach arguing buy coaching on someone else's platform reads as an ad no matter the hedging, while the identical argument lands clean in owned email where the invoice is already on the table. Last, a few pitches were algorithm statistics wearing a thin idea, and the lesson for future sessions is that platform mechanics justify a format but never substitute for the concept inside it. Next time, assign each persona an angle rather than a topic, and ask of every external pitch whose ground it stands on.

## If you only make three things this month

**The Most Skilled Thing That Happened at Rampage Was Nobody Riding**, because the peg is perishable, the thread is the hottest in the sport, and the decision-science lane is still unoccupied, but only for a few more weeks.

**A Smoother Trail Is a Worse Teacher**, because the sanitization debate and the NSMBA campaign window (November 1 to January 31) are both live right now, it is the best thought-leadership position on the slate, and it costs one essay with no dependencies.

**The Opening Take**, because it is the cheapest structural change available, it compounds twice a week from the first send, and its click data quietly tells you which of everything else above to make next.
