# Content Think Tank: sportsense, July 28, 2026

The niche is at its annual attention peak. Crankworx Whistler is running right now (July 24 to August 2), the Canadian Open Enduro was decided in the rain on July 26, and the World Cup midseason storylines are still hot: Vali Höll became the first elite woman since 2016 to take five wins in a season at Andorra on July 11, Jordan Williams won back-to-back rounds, and Whistler hosts its first ever UCI Downhill World Cup on September 25 to 27, which gives the calendar a guaranteed second peak. Two structural shifts matter more than any race result. First, AI self-coaching arrived: BikeFit AI has been measuring joint angles from phone video for free since April 2026 and MTBCoach targets a launch within months, so every coach is about to be asked what a human adds on top of a free app. Second, the platforms now reward exactly what a founder account can produce: Instagram's July 2026 update boosts small original creators running repeatable named formats, sends per reach is the confirmed top signal, carousels are out-engaging Reels roughly two to one, and Google now indexes Instagram content while AI Overviews appear on 21.59 percent of mobile queries. Meanwhile the live Singletrack World coaching threads are stating sportsense's founding problem in the audience's own words: a day of coaching provides pointers, and value depends on whether you can keep on top of what you learned afterwards.

## The slate

### Blog

**Why didn't my skills clinic stick? The forums already answered.** Question-and-answer post, boldness 2. Opens by quoting the live Singletrack World coaching threads and agreeing with them completely, which is the disarming move, then names the mechanism the riders are describing without naming it: the practice gap between sessions is where clinics go to die. It walks through what a coach-guided feedback loop between visits looks like and only then mentions sportsense as the infrastructure that runs it, closing with a short FAQ block, and it launches as page one of a numbered question series with visible last-updated dates and a six-month refresh cadence so it stays inside the AI-citation freshness window (this merges the analyst and producer versions of the same post into the insider draft, which was the strongest). Hook: you paid for a skills day, rode home buzzing, and six weeks later you corner exactly the way you did before, and the riders on the forums have a theory about why. Why now: the threads are active this month and AI Overviews now reward exactly this question-then-answer structure. Critics: all three advanced it as the only version a coach could drop into the actual forum thread without embarrassment.

**The angle measurement was never the product.** Contrarian essay doubling as the definitive comparison page, boldness 3. The argument is that free angle measurement is good news for coaches because it exposes what athletes were paying for all along, which is a human who knows their history deciding which of the twelve visible errors matters first, sequencing the fix against a progression, and holding them accountable next week. This is now a single package: the essay is the page, the newsletter lead ("the angle measurement is free now") is the email wrapper that drives coaches to it, and the Short below is the video leg, with one scheduled six-month refresh keeping the whole thing citable rather than splitting equity across duplicate pages (merging the analyst email pitch and the thought-leader essay into one asset). Hook: a free app can now measure your knee angle from a phone clip, and if that sentence scares you as a coach, you have been selling the wrong thing. Why now: BikeFit AI shipped in April, MTBCoach lands within months, and whoever publishes the answer first owns the citation. Critics: advanced on the condition it gets built once, not twice.

**Does filming yourself riding actually help? Ask a golfer.** Cross-sport question-and-answer essay, boldness 4. Walks through the mature machinery of amateur golf and the paid VOD-review economy in esports, then turns to mountain biking, where riders spend $850 on a clinic day and never point a camera at a corner, and answers plainly why the gap existed (golf has a fixed camera and a repeatable rep, MTB needed overlay, comparison, and a coach in the loop) and why it just closed. The title is reframed around the question riders actually ask and the tone runs on curiosity rather than indictment, since the riders described are also the audience. Hook: a golfer with a 25 handicap films every swing without thinking twice, while a rider who just spent $850 on a skills clinic goes home and never films a single corner. Why now: the ZEP anniversary feature just declared video feedback the profession's new normal. Critics: the mechanism section is what turns it from shame content into an argument that gets cited.

**A free app just measured my knee angle. Now what?** 30 to 45 second Short, boldness 4. ### Instagram

**Guess the Stage: a coach reads a rider he has never met.** Named recurring Reels format, boldness 4, the highest-rated idea in the room. Followers submit a raw clip, Rich watches it cold and places the rider on the skill progression in under a minute, narrating the exact movement cues that gave them away, and the episode ends on the reveal of the rider's own self-assessment against Rich's placement, which is the tension engine the Guess the Elo original runs on. It launches monthly rather than weekly, the first episodes are seeded with Rich's own athletes' clips with permission until the submission pipeline exists, and each episode closes with a comment-keyword prompt that delivers the relevant drill progression by DM (this merges the two duplicate cold-read pitches into the version that kept the reveal). Hook: I have never met this rider, and by the end of this clip I will tell you what they learned last month and what they should learn next. Why now: judged-submission formats are proven at massive scale in chess and esports, and the July algorithm update rewards exactly this kind of owned repeatable structure from small accounts. Critics: two advances at 9, with every episode a live demonstration of remote video analysis that never pitches.

**The sentence every coach has heard word for word.** Single-take Reel plus a paired carousel, boldness 2. Opens on trailside footage with one line of text, "I love training with you but I cannot come more often," then Rich in one take: if you coach, you did not just read that sentence, you heard it, in a specific voice, in a specific car park. The argument is that the sentence is not a goodbye but an unbuilt product, and the mechanism lands 24 hours later in a follow-up carousel showing what a remote tier actually contains, timed to catch the carousel re-serve window while the Reel is still warming the audience (this folds the producer's two-stroke distribution mechanic into the insider script, retiring the two weaker versions). Hook: the nicest thing an athlete ever says to you is also the sentence that ends the relationship. Why now: sends per reach is the confirmed top signal and this is engineered to be forwarded coach to coach. Critics: the car park line is the recognition moment that triggers the forward.

**The $850 clinic and the math nobody in the thread is doing.** 8 to 10 slide carousel, boldness 3. Slide one is a real rider's verbatim complaint from this month's clinic threads, taken seriously rather than argued with, and the reframe is the unit of purchase: you are not buying a day, you are buying a change in how you ride, so the honest metric is cost per skill that sticks. The math runs one clinic day with no follow-up against a modest monthly remote tier with the same coach, stays generous toward the quoted rider throughout, refers to "a $750 clinic" without naming the businesses (clinic-running coaches are exactly who sportsense is courting), and ends coach-facing (merging the weaker analyst version into this one). Hook: after $3,000 on the bike, $850 for two days of coaching makes it not worth it, a real rider wrote that this month, and he is doing the math wrong in an interesting way. Why now: sticker shock is live and quotable at peak clinic season, and carousels are the engagement format of 2026. Critics: three advances, with the verbatim quote doing the work.

**They are not leaning back (myth vs mechanism, episode one).** Named recurring Reels format, boldness 3. One perennially argued technique myth per episode, settled with skeletal overlay, angle measurement, and side-by-side comparison rather than opinion, starting with the lean-back debate running on MTBR right now. Each myth is demonstrated on Rich's own demonstrator and skill-library clips rather than pro broadcast footage, which the overlay makes just as convincing, and each episode closes with a comment-keyword prompt that delivers the drill progression by DM (absorbing the CTA from the killed duplicate); flat cornering is episode two, since the forum argument bank refills itself. Hook: this rider looks like he is leaning back, measure it and he is not, and the difference is the whole argument. Why now: error-framed hooks are the overperforming mid-2026 pattern and the format demos the analysis tools without naming a feature. Critics: called the cleanest native product demo on the slate once the footage source moved in-house.

**The software fee that grows every time you succeed.** 8 to 10 slide carousel, boldness 3. Walks the per-athlete pricing math one slide at a time the way a finance creator walks a tax form: thirty athletes at $9 each is $243 a month before the base fee, a bill that rises every time your coaching gets better, then flips the model to infrastructure where an added athlete is added recurring revenue. ### Email

**A level 4 certificate and an empty January.** Founder-voice newsletter lead, boldness 4. The profession is investing heavily in credentials in 2026 while its own career guidance still describes a seasonal, hourly-capped trade, and the argument is that certification makes you a better coach and does nothing for the shape of your income, with the remote tier as the missing business-model curriculum. Rich owns his position inside the certification world explicitly in the copy, because that insider standing is the credibility if it is stated rather than hidden, and warm leads get one CTA: a look at what a month of remote coaching actually looks like. Hook, subject line: the industry taught you to coach, nobody taught you to get paid in February. Why now: the credential-versus-income gap has never been easier to point at. Critics: three advances, the best subject line on the slate, and the one argument only Rich can make without being dismissed.

**What riders say about coaches when no coach is in the thread.** Founder-voice email, boldness 4. Built from the value-of-coaching threads, quoting (anonymized) the lines coaches never hear directly, and landing on the read that riders are not skeptical of coaching, they are skeptical of unverifiable coaching, which makes structured written video feedback the one artifact that proves a coach was not winging it and keeps proving it between sessions. The single CTA invites a reply about the thing an athlete said that stuck, which generates outreach conversations and future content in the same stroke. Hook, subject line: worth it with a good coach, not so much with a crap coach. Why now: the quality-variance fear is the sharpest live thread while the profession publicly professionalizes. Critics: the most naturally engaging email on the slate with a quietly structural reply mechanic.

**One real week: the remote coaching ledger.** Document-walkthrough newsletter pilot, boldness 5, a merge of the two open-books pitches into the version one person can run. One actual week of one consenting coach's remote tier shown as artifacts: the real athlete submission, the real annotated feedback with the overlay visible, the real message exchange, and the real Stripe line items against the hours it took, because receipts are the one thing coaching software never shows and the numbers must not get rounded off in edit or it becomes every other case study. It runs as a single pilot edition (Rich's own roster if no volunteer exists) and only becomes a named series once a second coach consents. Hook, subject line: an athlete paid their coach this month and never booked a session. Why now: document-walkthrough concreteness is the top-performing format in adjacent niches and coaches are primed by per-athlete fee resentment. Critics: two advances for the nerve of the receipts, one kill for the unscoped series, resolved by piloting.

## Wildcards worth the risk

**The one-day skills clinic is the most expensive way to not get better** (Pinkbike editorial, boldness 5). The risk is real: this attacks the flagship product of the exact coaches sportsense wants to win, in the most combative comment section in the sport, through an editorial door Rich does not control. But the position is precisely calibrated, defending the coach while attacking the business model they inherited, and the riders have already delivered the verdict in their own threads, so the piece is agreeing with the market rather than lecturing it. Run it without naming any clinic business, keep sportsense nearly invisible in the text, pitch it on the ZEP anniversary peg, and publish on the owned blog if Pinkbike passes. If it lands, it starts the industry argument with sportsense's thesis sitting at the center of it, which no safe piece can buy.

**I raced the free app: same clip, two analyses, you pick** (Short, boldness 5). Platforming your own substitute on screen and inviting the audience to judge is genuinely dangerous, and the room split hard on it: the audience could pick the free app out of spite, and the contest reads rigged if Rich judges it himself. The skeptic's counterpoint is the reason to keep it alive: the stated risk of losing on camera is the entire credibility, and the safe agree-then-turn version in the slate proves the stakes are the idea. Hold it until the insider Short has run, then ship this as the escalation, ideally with the featured rider (not Rich) saying which analysis would change their next session, which removes the rigged-judge problem while keeping the nerve.

**Deconstructed: a trick, layer by layer** (Reels format, boldness 4). The stems-deconstruction structure from music production is a genuinely fresh import nobody in MTB uses: a finished stylish move stripped back to its three or four progression layers, each shown as its own skill-library clip before the full move plays with everything stacked. The pitched version dies on rights and timing (Crankworx footage, an August 2 peg), so the risk in the rebuilt version is different: without a famous hero clip the hook carries less borrowed heat, and the format lives or dies on whether Rich's own advanced riding is impressive enough to open it. It probably is, and if the format works it is the most elegant athlete-facing argument for the progression library on the slate, made entirely without naming a feature.

## Killed, and why

Almost everything that died fell into one of three traps. The biggest was self-duplication: the room pitched the same five concepts (the sentence Reel, the clinic-stick post, the cold-read format, the two-seconds Short, the myth-buster) two and three times each, and in every cluster the copies that died were the ones missing the single load-bearing element, the self-assessment reveal, the verbatim rider quote, the car park line, so future sessions should pool around one version early and spend the saved pitches on new ground. The second was footage rights: every idea built structurally on World Cup or Crankworx broadcast material was undeliverable on a commercial account, and the fix was the same each time, since the overlay is just as convincing on owned and athlete-supplied clips, which the originality rules reward anyway. The third was posture and scope: content that shames the audience it wants ("the last athletes who refuse to watch themselves") violates the respect rule regardless of how true it is, news pegs that expire before a solo founder can ship are not pegs, and any format specced beyond what one person can sustain weekly is a treadmill, not a series.

## If you only make three things this month

1. **Guess the Stage** (Instagram): the highest-rated idea in the room, an owned recurring format the algorithm now explicitly rewards, and every episode is the product demonstrating itself on athlete-supplied footage without a single pitch.
2. **Why didn't my skills clinic stick?** (blog): the citable page the entire Reddit and AEO strategy points at, published while the forum threads it quotes are still live, with the refresh system that keeps it cited for years.
3. **A level 4 certificate and an empty January** (email): the sharpest coach-acquisition argument on the slate, aimed at the exact revenue-motivated buyer, and the one piece only Rich can credibly send.
